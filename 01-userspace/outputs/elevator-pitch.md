# OUT-ELEVATOR: Elevator pitch

Purpose: the shortest statement of the idea that a stranger can act on, generated when the vault has something worth saying in under a minute of reading.

## Word budget: 60 to 120 words

A document in this kind outside the budget is not a shorter idea: it is a different kind of document filed in the wrong place.

## Sections

1. **The idea, in one sentence** - what it is, in the terms the owner would use with a stranger.
2. **Why it matters now** - the change, need, or problem it answers, in at most two sentences.
3. **What is already settled** - the strongest two or three claims the vault holds as `accepted`.
4. **What is still open** - one line naming what is not decided, with the number of open questions rather than their contents.

## Draws from

`10-idea/` for the statement, `20-claims/` for what is settled, `01-userspace/questions/` for the count of what is open.

## May claim

Only what the vault holds. A claim still `proposed` is stated as held, not as settled, and a `draft` claim is left out rather than softened.

## Must cite

Its kind and the notes it drew from, in frontmatter. No internal IDs, no folder names, no namespace prefixes in the readable body: this document is for a reader who has not read the vault.

## Not for

Anything that needs qualification to be true. A pitch that has to explain its own caveats is a brief, and belongs in `OUT-BRIEF`.

## Evidence

The budget is stated, not measured: 60 to 120 words is roughly one minute of reading, and a document longer than that stops being something a reader can hold in memory.
